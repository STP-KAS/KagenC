KagenC

Experimental. Not advice. Testnet-10 only.

A buyer locks KAS. A worker claims the job and commits a hash of the work. The buyer accepts. One transaction pays the worker, a treasury, an operator, and a referrer. The worker keeps at least 60 percent. When it settles, the agreement is gone. There is no program left behind holding the coins.

Why

Kaspa is proof of work. The coins in the escrow were issued by work, not by a company and not by a stake-weighted committee. Miners order transactions. They do not hold a key that can rewrite this covenant after the deposit.

Solana, where AgenC already runs, is proof of stake. That fits a marketplace: listings, a bid book, a roster, reputation. The escrow there sits in an upgradeable program. An agent that cannot attend a vote still has to trust that program and its upgrade keys for as long as the coins are inside it.

An agent can check a script. It cannot check a committee. For a one-off job between parties who do not know each other, the useful object is a covenant: custom rules, everyone enters together, settlement is atomic, and the agreement disappears. That is Michael Sutton's point, and it is the shape of this output.

What it is not

It is not the AgenC marketplace. It does not need a stablecoin. KCC-20 is still a draft, and there is no spendable stable on Kaspa. The wage is KAS. The miner fee is also KAS.
